Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about prints, licensing, and working with me.

Do you sell fine art prints?

Yes. All prints are produced on archival-quality paper and available in multiple sizes. Each print is signed and shipped with a certificate of authenticity. You can browse available works in the Portfolio section.

How can I license a photo for editorial or commercial use?

Please get in touch via the Contact page with the intended use, publication, and timeframe. Licensing fees vary depending on usage rights, exclusivity, and duration. I'll respond within 2 business days.

Where are your photographs taken?

Most of my work is shot across Germany — from the Bavarian Alps and Black Forest to the North Sea coast and the Brandenburg lowlands. Location details are included in the metadata of each image on request.

Can I commission you for a project?

Yes, I'm available for commissioned nature and landscape work, editorial assignments, and long-term projects. Reach out through the Contact page with a brief description of your project and timeline.

How long does delivery take for a print order?

Prints are produced to order and typically shipped within 7–10 business days. Delivery within Germany takes 2–3 days. International shipping is available and usually takes 5–10 business days depending on destination.

What camera equipment do you use?

I shoot with a full-frame mirrorless system paired with a range of prime and zoom lenses suited for low-light and long-distance wildlife work. I also carry a lightweight kit for multi-day hikes. Happy to discuss gear in more detail — just ask.

What is the best time of day for nature photography?

The golden hour — the first hour after sunrise and the last hour before sunset — gives the softest, warmest light and the most dramatic shadows. Early morning also tends to be quieter: wildlife is more active, mist sits low on the landscape, and you have the location to yourself. That said, overcast days are underrated for forest and macro work, where diffused light reduces harsh contrasts.

How do you approach wildlife without disturbing the animals?

Patience and preparation are everything. I research behavior and habitats before arriving, move slowly and stay low, wear neutral colors, and use a long telephoto lens so I can keep a respectful distance. I never bait, call, or otherwise manipulate animals for a shot — the welfare of the subject always comes before the photograph.

How do you handle difficult weather conditions in the field?

Weather is often an asset rather than an obstacle. Storms, fog, and snow create atmosphere that clear days rarely offer. I use weather-sealed gear and keep everything in waterproof bags. The key is knowing when conditions are genuinely dangerous versus just uncomfortable — and being willing to pack up and come back another day.

Do you do post-processing on your images?

Yes, all images go through a careful editing process — adjusting exposure, contrast, color balance, and sharpness to reflect what I saw in the field. I aim for a natural look rather than heavy manipulation. I do not add or remove elements from a scene. What you see in the final image is what was there.

Any tips for someone just starting out in nature photography?

Start close to home — familiar locations let you focus on learning your camera instead of navigating new terrain. Shoot in RAW format from day one so you have full control in editing. Study the light more than the gear. And go out consistently, even when conditions seem uninspiring — the best shots often come on days you almost didn't bother.
